# Multiplier: agreements and platform documents plan (v1)

Status: planning document for drafting. Not legal advice. Every agreement below should be reviewed by a qualified lawyer before it is relied on, particularly the regulatory statements, the non-circumvention terms and the investor eligibility wording, which differ by country.

## 1. Relationship map

```
     (Listing Agreement + Product Schedule; pays all fees to Multiplier)
                       │  fees
                       ▼
                   MULTIPLIER  ──── pays 70% of Deal Fees (paid-when-paid) ───►  Adviser firm
           (technology platform;                                                   │  override from
            never holds investor money)  ◄── override paid from Multiplier's 30% ──┘  Multiplier's share
                       ▲                                                          to referring adviser
                       │  Platform Terms + Investor Terms + eligibility
                       │
                    Investor  ──── advice and suitability ────►  Adviser (the investor's own adviser)
                       │
                       └─── subscribes directly with Sponsor / administrator (money never passes through Multiplier)
```

Who is responsible for what (the contracts must line up with this):
- **Sponsor/issuer:** the product, its documents, its legal offering basis in each country, investor AML/KYC acceptance, investor relations, product complaints.
- **Adviser:** their own licence, suitability or appropriateness for their client, their client communications, disclosure of their remuneration, advice complaints.
- **Investor:** their own eligibility declarations, decisions and due diligence.
- **Multiplier:** the software, screening of sponsors to its published standard, gating access, keeping records, collecting fees and paying advisers.

## 2. What you have, and what you missed

### Advisers (Adviser Firm Agreement)
Your points: 70% of deal fees; override for introduced advisers, paid only from Multiplier's share; clawback; strict non-circumvention and non-solicitation of sponsors; no advice, guarantees or product responsibility from Multiplier; adviser does own due diligence.

Gaps to close:
1. **Define "Deal Fees" precisely.** Recommend: success fees actually received by Multiplier from the sponsor on funded and settled investments made by that adviser's clients, net of VAT and other taxes, refunds and chargebacks. **Exclude** screening and listing fees, or say so explicitly if you want to include them.
2. **Paid-when-paid and payment mechanics:** payment within [X] days of Multiplier receiving cleared funds; monthly statements; currency and FX rule; adviser invoices Multiplier or a **self-billing** arrangement; tax and VAT responsibility; withholding where required; bank details verified.
3. **Contract with the firm, not the individual.** The adviser firm signs; its individual users (relationship managers) accept user terms. Payments go to the firm unless the firm instructs otherwise and local rules allow.
4. **Regulatory warranties by the adviser:** licensed or authorised where they operate, permissions maintained, professional indemnity insurance, they treat their clients under their own conduct rules, they disclose remuneration to clients as their rules require, they comply with financial promotion rules, and they notify Multiplier of any regulatory action.
5. **AML responsibilities:** Multiplier onboards and screens the adviser firm (it is paying them). Investor AML stays with the sponsor or administrator, with any adviser reliance arrangement set out separately.
6. **Clawback detail:** triggers (sponsor refunds or claws back the fee; subscription cancelled or rescinded; investor redeems within [X] months; misconduct, misrepresentation or breach; regulatory finding or upheld complaint linked to the sale); method (set-off against future fees, otherwise repay within [30] days); optional **holdback** of [X]% for [X] months; survives termination.
7. **Override (referral) terms:** belongs in separate **Adviser Referral Programme Rules** (see section 3). Keep it to **one level only**: multi-level payouts look like a pyramid and attract scrutiny. Cap it so Multiplier's retained share never falls below [X]%. Define duration (for example [24] months from the introduced adviser's first funded deal), what happens if either adviser leaves, and that the override is disclosed where rules require.
8. **Non-circumvention and non-solicitation (sponsors):** cover direct or indirect dealings with any sponsor, issuer or product the adviser saw or was introduced to through Multiplier, during the term and for [24] months after; "deemed fee" payable as if the deal had gone through Multiplier, plus costs; injunction rights; audit or information right. Drafting note: these clauses are enforced only if reasonable in scope and length, so keep them tied to platform-introduced parties and a fixed period.
9. **What advisers will ask for in return (recommended):** a **client protection clause** stating that the adviser's clients remain the adviser's, and that Multiplier will not solicit them to bypass the adviser for [period]. This makes the agreement much easier to sign and supports your growth model.
10. **Marketing and content rules:** no guarantees, no claims about returns, no use of Multiplier's name beyond approved materials, no sharing of product documents outside the platform, no sub-introducers without consent.
11. **Data protection:** both parties as independent controllers for client data, with a data-sharing schedule and international-transfer clauses.
12. **Confidentiality, anti-bribery, sanctions, conflicts.**
13. **Points and leaderboard:** governed by separate **Points Rules**; points have no cash value; Multiplier may change or end the scheme. (Your preference for a commission-linked score should be reviewed by counsel before launch.)
14. **Independence:** no agency, partnership or employment; adviser cannot bind Multiplier.
15. **Liability and indemnity:** adviser indemnifies Multiplier for breaches, mis-selling and regulatory failures; Multiplier's liability capped at [fees paid in the prior 12 months].
16. **Term, termination and survival:** fees on deals funded before termination remain payable (subject to clawback); confidentiality, non-circumvention and clawback survive.
17. **Governing law and disputes:** recommend arbitration (for example LCIA, ICC or DIAC) for cross-border enforceability; seat [placeholder].

### Sponsors / issuers (Listing Agreement and Product Schedule)
Your points: protect Multiplier's fees; no circumvention or solicitation; prior relationships irrelevant if the deal goes through Multiplier.

Gaps to close:
1. **Fee structure in the agreement:** non-refundable **screening fee** (payable whether or not listed), **listing fee** [annual], **success fee** [rate card]% of funded capital from Platform Investors, payable within [X] days of each closing. Late interest. Fees not reduced by any payment the sponsor makes to anyone else.
2. **"Platform Investor" definition (this is what protects your slice):** any investor who registered on, was invited through, received or viewed the product on Multiplier, or was introduced by a Multiplier adviser, regardless of any earlier relationship with the sponsor.
3. **Tail period:** success fee also due on investments by Platform Investors for [24 to 36] months after first introduction, including later closings, top-ups, re-investments, roll-overs and successor or parallel vehicles, whether or not processed on the platform.
4. **Prior relationships:** state expressly that a prior relationship does not reduce fees on deals processed through Multiplier. Optional carve-out: a short, **pre-registered list** of existing investors who are excluded only for off-platform deals. Sponsors will ask for this, and it makes the clause easier to enforce.
5. **No direct dealing with Multiplier advisers:** sponsor must not pay, reward or contract with any platform adviser in respect of platform deals outside Multiplier, and must not solicit platform investors or advisers directly for [period]. Non-solicitation of Multiplier staff.
6. **Reporting and audit:** sponsor reports each closing (investor, amount, date) within [X] days; Multiplier has audit and information rights; fee true-up if under-reported.
7. **Sponsor warranties:** properly constituted and, where required, licensed; offering documents accurate and complete; lawful offering basis in each country where the product is made available (private placement, exemptions, investor categories), with **selling restrictions supplied to Multiplier** so the platform can gate access; no undisclosed litigation, sanctions or regulatory action; ongoing duty to notify material changes, defaults and breaches.
8. **AML/KYC:** the sponsor or its administrator makes the final investor acceptance decision; Multiplier's checks do not replace theirs.
9. **Subscription and money flow:** subscription monies go directly to the sponsor, escrow or administrator, never to Multiplier. The sponsor's product-specific subscription requirements are set out in the Product Schedule.
10. **Data feeds:** sponsor supplies capital calls, distributions, coupons, valuations and documents in the platform's format and timetable.
11. **Screening consent:** consent to background checks; Multiplier may refuse, suspend or delist at its discretion, including on adverse information, without liability.
12. **Content and IP licence:** right to display logos and documents; sponsor responsible for their accuracy and approvals.
13. **Investor relations and complaints:** product complaints sit with the sponsor.
14. **Indemnity:** for inaccurate materials, unlawful offering, breach of selling restrictions and investor claims about the product. Multiplier's liability capped.
15. **Acknowledgements:** Multiplier does not underwrite, recommend or endorse the product; no guarantee of any amount raised; Multiplier pays advisers from its fees and will disclose remuneration as required.
16. **Data protection, confidentiality, anti-bribery, sanctions, governing law and arbitration, term, termination and survival** (fees and tail survive).

### Investors (Platform Terms, Investor Terms and declarations)
Your points: self-certification by country; reverse solicitation; no advice; technology platform, no recourse, not responsible for investments, own due diligence.

Gaps and corrections:
1. **Self-certification is not enough everywhere.** Several regimes require a specific form of words, evidence, an opt-in, or certification by an authorised person (for example the UK sophisticated or high net worth statements, Singapore accredited investor opt-in, Malaysia sophisticated investor categories, Brazil qualified or professional investor, UAE professional client). Plan a **country-by-country eligibility module** with placeholders for each wording, evidence and refresh period.
2. **Reverse solicitation: use with care.** Regulators read it narrowly. It applies only where the investor genuinely approached first. A tick-box after the platform or an adviser has approached the investor is unlikely to hold, and could look misleading. Recommend: record genuine reverse solicitation only where true (a free-text or attestation step), and rely mainly on eligibility gating and the sponsor's offering basis.
3. **"Not regulated, no recourse":** describe what Multiplier does and does not do, rather than a blanket "not regulated" statement, which must be accurate in every country you serve. Exclusions apply "to the fullest extent permitted by law": some liabilities (fraud, and in many places consumer protections) cannot be excluded. Avoid "no recourse" language that a court could treat as unfair.
4. **Remuneration disclosure:** investors should be told that Multiplier receives fees from sponsors and shares them with advisers. This is required in many places and protects the whole model.
5. **The adviser's role:** where the investor has an adviser, suitability and advice come from that adviser, not Multiplier.
6. **Risk acknowledgement** per product (capital at risk, illiquidity, documents read, no guarantee).
7. **AML and tax declarations:** source of funds and wealth, PEP status, sanctions, tax residency and CRS/FATCA self-certification, **not a US person**.
8. **Data sharing consent:** with the investor's adviser and with sponsors they choose to subscribe to.
9. **Data-room confidentiality undertaking** per product.
10. **Electronic signatures and communications consent.**
11. **Complaints routing:** product to sponsor, advice to adviser, platform to Multiplier.

## 4. Signing and records (how the platform should handle it)

- **Three acceptance tiers:** click-wrap (terms and policies), click-wrap with explicit checkbox (investor terms and declarations), and full e-signature with audit trail and Multiplier countersignature (adviser and sponsor agreements, schedules, certificates).
- For every acceptance, store: document ID and version, hash of the text, user, firm, timestamp, IP address, and a PDF copy. Show users their signed documents.
- **Re-acceptance** when a document changes materially; block platform use until accepted.
- Some documents may need stronger signatures in certain countries (for example powers of attorney or notarised forms). Keep the e-signature provider able to do qualified or advanced signatures later.
- Never let a product open until the right documents for that user, product and country are complete.
