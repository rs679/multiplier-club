# Investor Eligibility Declaration

```
Document ID: INV-02
Version: 0.1 (draft for legal review)
Audience: Investors (individuals, joint investors, and Authorised Users acting for companies, trusts, family offices and funds)
Presented when: With the Investor Terms (INV-01), or at the latest before the Investor first opens a Product data room; again every [PLACEHOLDER: 12] months, or sooner if the country module requires it or the Investor's circumstances change
Accepted by: e-signature (form plus e-signature; evidence upload and third-party certification where the country module requires it)
Unlocks: Opening data rooms for Products whose eligible countries and investor categories match the Investor's declared country and category
Re-acceptance: At the end of each refresh period; on a material change to this form or to the relevant country module; when the Investor reports a change of circumstances
```

> Drafting note: This declaration is built in modules. Every investor completes Part A (general). The Platform then shows only the country module (Part B) that matches the investor's country of residence or, for an entity, its country of registration. Part C (reverse solicitation) is optional and appears only if the investor chooses to open it. Part D (declarations and signature) applies to everyone.
>
> Self-certification is not enough in several countries. Some regimes require a set form of words, supporting evidence, an opt-in after a written warning, or an assessment or certificate by an authorised firm. Each module therefore has six fields: category name(s); statement or wording; evidence required; certification by an authorised person or adviser; opt-in; and refresh period. All thresholds, statutory references and prescribed wording are placeholders. Counsel in each country must confirm or replace them before that country goes live. Where we have named a category or regime, it is to help counsel locate the right rules and must be confirmed.
>
> Platform logic: the Platform should compare the investor's confirmed category with the eligible countries and investor categories in each Product Listing Schedule, and open a data room only where they match. Where a module needs evidence or third-party certification, the investor's status should show as "pending" until it is received and checked, and data rooms should stay closed.

## How this form works (shown to the investor)

Private-market investments on the Platform are only for investors who meet certain conditions in their country, such as professional, sophisticated, accredited, qualified or high net worth investors.

This form asks you to tell us which category you fall into. In some countries the law also asks for a particular statement, for evidence, or for a regulated firm to confirm your status. Where that applies, we will tell you what is needed.

Please answer carefully and truthfully. Sponsors, your Adviser and Multiplier rely on your answers to decide what you may see. Your answers do not mean that any Product is suitable for you.

It should take about [PLACEHOLDER: 5] minutes. You will need to renew it every [PLACEHOLDER: 12] months.

## Part B: Country modules

### How each module is set out

Each module below uses the same six fields:

| Field | What it means |
|---|---|
| Category name(s) | The investor categories recognised for that country. The investor chooses one. |
| Statement or wording | The words the investor must sign. Where the law prescribes wording, it must be inserted verbatim. |
| Evidence required | Documents the investor must upload, if any. |
| Certification by authorised person or adviser | Whether a licensed firm (for example the investor's Adviser, the Sponsor's distributor or another authorised person) must assess or certify the investor's status. |
| Opt-in required | Whether the investor must actively choose to be treated in this category, usually after a written warning about protections they lose. |
| Refresh period | How long the declaration lasts before it must be renewed. |

Every module also includes a "None of these apply to me" option. If chosen, the investor cannot open data rooms and is shown: "Based on your answers, we cannot currently show you Product details. If your circumstances change, you can update this form."

## Part C: Optional record that you approached first (reverse solicitation)

This part is optional. It is hidden by default. The investor must choose to open it.

**Please read this before you continue.**

> Only complete this section if it is true that **you** contacted a Sponsor, an Adviser or Multiplier **on your own initiative**, before anyone contacted you, about the specific type of investment or Product described below.

> **Do not** complete this section if:
> - an Adviser, a Sponsor, Multiplier or anyone acting for them contacted you first about this type of investment or Product;

C4. Date of your first contact: [date]

C5. How you made contact (for example email, phone call, meeting): [free text]

> Drafting note (reverse solicitation): Regulators read reverse solicitation narrowly. It is generally only available where the investor's approach was genuinely unsolicited, and it usually covers only the product or service the investor asked about. EU authorities have said that firms cannot rely on a contractual clause or a standard declaration to show reverse solicitation. For that reason: (1) this Part must never be pre-ticked, mandatory, or presented as a step needed to proceed; (2) Advisers and Sponsors must not prompt investors to complete it; (3) the Platform should record the timestamp, the investor's own words and any evidence; (4) Multiplier's compliance function should review entries and be able to reject them; and (5) Products should not be opened to an investor on the strength of this Part alone unless counsel has confirmed that is permissible in the relevant country. The primary basis for access remains Part B and the Sponsor's offering basis.
