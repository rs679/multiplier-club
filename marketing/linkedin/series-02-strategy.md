# Multiplier on LinkedIn: persona strategy and posts to the end of 2026

Thirteen posts from Tuesday 18 November to Tuesday 30 December: one opener, then four for each persona, rotating so each audience hears from us roughly every ten days. Graphics are in `png/series-02/`, built from `src/series-02.html`. Captions follow each strategy.

Ground rules for every post: no returns, yields, fee rates or performance; no claim that a deal is checked, safe or endorsed; no invented numbers about the platform (advisers, deals, countries signed up); every product screen is fictional and labelled. Investors are not addressed: Multiplier does not promote to them.

How we will know it works: the website application form records which kind of applicant arrived (individual adviser, adviser firm, sponsor). Read the Applications page weekly against the posting calendar; that is the scorecard, not likes.

## Persona 1: the sponsor raising capital

**Who.** Founder or partner of a mid-market fund manager, real estate developer, private credit lender or operating company raising USD 5 to 100 million. Often in the Gulf, London, Switzerland or Singapore. Has raised before, mostly from their own network and a handful of family offices.

**What they want.** Capital from beyond their own contacts, without giving a placement agent exclusivity, and without losing control of who presents the deal or on what terms. Speed: every month of a raise costs them.

**What frustrates them.** Distribution through advisers happens one relationship at a time: an NDA, a deck, a call, a follow-up, repeated fifty times, with fifty slightly different versions of the story in circulation and no idea who has seen what. Advisers ask the same questions. Confidential deals leak.

**What they must believe to sign up.** 1. Advisers on Multiplier are real, serve wealthy clients and can be reached without one-by-one introductions. 2. They keep control: countries, client categories, who is approved, the terms. 3. The deal stays confidential until they say yes. 4. Listing is not another month of paperwork.

**Strategy.** Lead with the outcome (reach), then answer the three objections in order: chaos, control, confidentiality. Tone: peer to peer, commercially sharp, no hype.

**Call to action.** "List your raise: multiplier.club" (the sponsor application).

## Persona 2: the independent adviser

**Who.** An adviser who works alone or with one or two colleagues: often a former private banker or relationship manager who left a large institution and kept the client relationships. Licensed in their jurisdiction (DIFC, ADGM, FCA, FINMA, MAS and others). Their clients are high-net-worth families and business owners.

**What they want.** Differentiated private market opportunities for their clients, the kind their old bank's shelf had. Revenue from what they place. To look as credible as the institution they left. To spend time with clients, not chasing sponsors.

**What frustrates them.** Sponsors do not return calls from a one-person firm. Terms take weeks of emails. Deal materials are written for institutions, not for a client conversation. They feel small.

**What they must believe to sign up.** 1. They get access as a sole adviser, on the same terms as a firm. 2. Getting terms is fast and does not depend on a sponsor answering emails. 3. The material is good enough to put in front of a client. 4. It costs them nothing but their time to look.

**Strategy.** Speak to identity first (the post that makes an ex-banker stop scrolling), then speed, then client-readiness, then credibility. Tone: respectful, a little personal, never patronising.

**Call to action.** "Request access as an adviser: multiplier.club".

## Persona 3: the adviser firm

**Who.** Managing partner or head of investments at a wealth manager, multi-family office or distributor with five to fifty advisers. Responsible for the firm's product offering, its compliance exposure and its revenue.

**What they want.** A private markets offering that keeps clients from drifting to the banks, without building a product team. Oversight of what their advisers offer. Simple contracting. Protection of the firm's own client relationships.

**What frustrates them.** Every adviser has their own sponsor contacts, their own spreadsheets and their own side agreements. Nobody can say at a glance which deals the firm is exposed to. Hiring a private markets specialist is expensive and slow.

**What they must believe to sign up.** 1. Multiplier works as an outsourced private markets desk. 2. One agreement covers the team, and the firm stays in control of who joins. 3. They can see everything the firm holds. 4. Their client book is never exposed to sponsors or to us.

**Strategy.** Lead with the strategic case (a new service line), then the operating answers: contracting, oversight, discretion. Tone: boardroom, measured, numerate.

**Call to action.** "Bring your firm onto the Exchange: multiplier.club".
